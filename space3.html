⭐ A Spoonful of a Neutron Star Weighs Billions of Tons

Published: SicFacts Space Desk | Astrophysics

Neutron stars are among the densest objects in the universe.

They are formed when massive stars explode in supernova events and collapse under their own gravity.

The matter inside a neutron star is packed so tightly that a single teaspoon of it would weigh billions of tons on Earth.

Despite being only about 20 kilometers wide, a neutron star can contain more mass than our Sun.

The gravity on its surface is so powerful that it would crush almost anything instantly.

Studying neutron stars helps scientists understand the limits of matter and the extreme conditions that exist in the universe.
